HABITATS

Sharks are ocean generalists.

There is no single “shark habitat.” Species have evolved to exploit very different parts of the marine environment.

☀︎

Coastal shallows

Reefs, bays, continental shelves and estuaries support species adapted to complex, productive environments.

Examples: nurse, bull, reef sharks
≈

Open ocean

Pelagic species travel through vast stretches of water, following prey and favorable conditions.

Examples: blue, mako, oceanic whitetip
↓

Deep sea

Low light, high pressure and scarce food create a very different world for deep-water sharks.

Examples: lantern, goblin sharks
❄

Cold waters

Some sharks tolerate very cold temperatures and have slow metabolisms and growth.

Example: Greenland shark

ECOLOGY

Every species is part of a food web.

Sharks can be predators, scavengers and filter feeders. Their ecological roles depend on species, life stage, location and prey availability. Removing or reducing shark populations can alter food webs, but the magnitude and direction of those effects vary by ecosystem.
