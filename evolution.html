DEEP TIME

A lineage older than dinosaurs.

Shark-like chondrichthyans have a fossil record extending hundreds of millions of years. The story is branching, incomplete and still being revised as new fossils and analyses appear.

~450 Ma

Early shark-lineage relatives

Fossils preserve early members of the shark and cartilaginous-fish story. The exact relationships of many ancient forms remain debated.

~400–350 Ma

Devonian & Carboniferous diversification

Shark diversity expands dramatically. Some extinct forms look surprisingly different from living sharks.

66 Ma

End-Cretaceous extinction

A global ecological upheaval eliminates many marine and terrestrial lineages. Shark groups survive and diversify afterward.

Today

500+ living species

Modern sharks occupy coastal, pelagic, polar and deep-sea environments, with feeding strategies ranging from filter feeding to active predation.

IMPORTANT CONTEXT

“Living fossil” is misleading.

Some sharks retain ancient-looking traits, but living species have continued to evolve. Their evolutionary history is a branching tree, not a straight line from ancient shark to modern shark.
